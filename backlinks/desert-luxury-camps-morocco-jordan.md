## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are luxury desert camps in Wadi Rum or the Moroccan Sahara worth the money?

They can be, but most people judge them on the wrong things. I review resorts and spas for a living, and at desert camps the bed, the shower and the dinner rarely decide whether a night is great or disappointing. What you are really paying for is distance from the next camp.

Here is an example. In February 2024 I paid $310 for a transparent dome outside Rum village in Jordan. The dome was lovely. At 2 a.m. I could hear a generator, someone's Bluetooth speaker playing Dua Lipa, and voices from a neighboring camp. Many premium camps in Wadi Rum, and around Merzouga in Morocco's Erg Chebbi, sit close together near access tracks because water, food and fuel are easier to bring in there. The photos show empty dunes. In person it can feel like a campground with nicer linens.

Before booking, I now ask four things:

1. How far away is the nearest other camp? Ask for a distance, not adjectives like 'secluded.'
2. Is power solar or generator, and if it is a generator, what time does it switch off?
3. How long is the 4x4 transfer from the last paved road? Longer usually means quieter.
4. What is the policy on music and group noise?

If the answers are vague, the price is probably paying for the decor rather than the silence. In Morocco, camps in Erg Chigaga, a longer drive from M'Hamid, tend to be more isolated than those at Erg Chebbi, though you give up some convenience for it.
