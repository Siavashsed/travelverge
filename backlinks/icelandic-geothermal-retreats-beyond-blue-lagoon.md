## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the best alternatives to the Blue Lagoon in Iceland for a quieter, more luxurious geothermal experience?

I've reviewed spas and resorts firsthand for years, and my honest answer is to stop looking for a better day spa and look for a hotel with its own hot water.

The Blue Lagoon is striking, but it's built for volume. You book a timed entry and share the water with tour groups. Then you leave on someone else's schedule. That's fine for a layover, but it isn't a retreat.

The best geothermal experiences I've had in Iceland were at properties a few hours from Keflavík that tap their own geothermal sources. Because the pools serve hotel guests rather than day visitors, everything changes. The water is quiet late at night, nobody watches the clock, and you sit in it surrounded by real landscape instead of a car park.

One example is Highland Base Kerlingarfjöll in the central highlands. In late September 2023 I sat in the pools there with the air at 2°C. There were four other bathers, and the sky was going violet over the ridges. It felt nothing like a crowded lagoon, and you can't buy that kind of quiet with a premium ticket at a mass-market venue.

A few practical points if you go this route:

1. Plan to drive, or book a transfer. Remoteness is the whole point, so build travel time into your itinerary.
2. Stay at least one night. The magic happens after the day crowds would normally arrive and leave, especially in the evening and late at night.
3. Go in shoulder season. September and early autumn bring cold air, warm water and a real chance of northern lights.
4. Check what each property's pools actually are. Some are natural, some engineered, and the experience differs.
