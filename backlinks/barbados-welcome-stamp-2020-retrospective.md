## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is the Barbados Welcome Stamp worth it for remote workers?

Short answer: for the right person, yes. Just be clear about what you are signing up for.

The Welcome Stamp launched in July 2020 and lets you live and work in Barbados for 12 months. The fee was $2,000 for an individual and $3,000 for a family, and you need to show at least $50,000 a year in income earned outside the country. The terms have changed since launch, so check the official government portal before you apply.

I've reviewed a lot of properties on the island, and these are the things I would weigh:

Where you stay matters more than the visa. A resort that feels perfect for a week can wear thin over several months. Look for reliable fibre internet (ask for a speed test, not a promise), a real desk, a kitchen and a monthly rate. Many west coast properties now offer long-stay packages. Negotiate, because a guest who stays a year is valuable to them.

Budget honestly. Barbados is not a cheap island. Groceries, imported goods and car hire add up, and the luxury end of the west coast is priced to match. The $50,000 threshold is a minimum, not a comfortable budget for that lifestyle.

Think about your daily rhythm. The time zone works well for North American and UK clients, and the island is small enough that a routine settles in fast. Some people love that. Others get island fever by month four.

Tax is your homework. Barbados does not tax your foreign income under the stamp, but your home country may still. Speak to an adviser.
