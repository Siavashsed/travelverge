## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I choose a spa retreat that will actually help me rest and sleep better?

I review resorts and spas for a living, and the most useful lesson I've learned is this: judge a retreat by how little it books you for, not how much.

In March 2024 I paid about $2,300 for four nights at a hillside hotel in Tuscany, with a program of eleven treatments. I came home with glowing shoulders and a grey, flat exhaustion. I'd spent four days moving between appointments with almost no free time. It was pleasant, and it was not restful.

Here is what I look for now.

1. Unbooked hours. Ask how many hours a day are genuinely free. If the schedule is full from breakfast to dinner, you're buying a timetable, not a retreat.

2. The bed and the room. Ask about mattress quality, blackout curtains, noise at night, and room temperature control. This matters more than the treatment menu, because you'll spend a third of your stay there.

3. Flexible packages. Avoid programs that lock in a fixed number of treatments. Two well-chosen treatments and a lot of empty time beat eleven rushed ones.

4. Quiet by design. Look for places that limit group classes, keep common areas calm, and don't send alarms or reminders to your phone.

5. Honest answers. A good spa will tell you when you'd be better off doing less. If the staff push the biggest package, treat that as a warning.

Treatments are easy to price and photograph, which is why spas sell them. Sleep is harder to put in a brochure, but it's usually what people actually came for.
