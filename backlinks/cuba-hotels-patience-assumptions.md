## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the best hotels to stay in Havana, Cuba, and are the new ones worth the price?

Short answer: in Havana, the newest hotels are rarely the best ones. I review resorts and spas for a living, and Cuba is one of the few places where I've seen the old guard beat the glass towers consistently.

I learned this the hard way. In March 2024 I paid about $410 a night for a corner room at a gleaming new Old Havana property. I arrived at 4 p.m. in the heat, and there was no hot water until 9 that night. The lobby photographed beautifully. The basics just weren't reliable yet.

Here is what I'd recommend instead.

1. Look at the 1930s grand dames. These hotels have the scale, the high ceilings, and the staff experience that newer buildings can't fake. They tend to cost less than the new luxury openings.

2. Consider restored colonial buildings. A handful in Old Havana are genuinely well done, with courtyards, period details, and a real sense of place.

3. Judge on operations, not appearance. Ask directly about hot water, air conditioning, generator backup, and check-in times before you book. Cuba's infrastructure is unpredictable, and properties with long track records have usually built in workarounds.

4. Arrive with patience. Things can run late, and a flexible attitude improves almost every stay.

5. Don't equate price with reliability. My $410 night was one of the weakest of my trip, while some far cheaper rooms were excellent.
