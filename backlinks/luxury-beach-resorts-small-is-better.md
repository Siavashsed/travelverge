## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why are small luxury beach resorts often better than large ones?

After years of reviewing resorts and spas firsthand, my short answer is this: luxury is attention, and attention doesn't scale.

The clearest example I have is from a four-night stay in March 2024 at a 38-villa property on a Maldivian atoll. I mentioned once, at check-in, that I take oat milk in my coffee. On the second morning at 6:40 a.m., my butler set down exactly that without being asked. A 400-room resort can have beautiful systems, but it rarely has the human bandwidth to make that happen reliably.

Here is what tends to separate small resorts (roughly under 60 rooms) from large ones:

1. Staffing ratio. Small properties often run two or more staff per villa or room. That's what produces anticipation rather than just responsiveness.

2. Honesty about the setting. The best beach resorts tell you about reef condition, tides, seasonal weather and seaweed before you arrive. Larger resorts often rely on a single polished photo.

3. Quiet. Fewer guests means an empty beach at 10 a.m., no scramble for loungers and no queue at breakfast.

4. Consistency of memory. Small teams know who you are by day two. Large teams reset every shift.

5. Design that serves the place. A lobby full of marble and a high thread count are decoration. They don't make a stay feel luxurious if service is generic.

That doesn't mean every small resort is excellent. Some are just expensive and understaffed, so check reviews for specifics about service, not adjectives. Ask directly about staff numbers and water conditions when you book.
