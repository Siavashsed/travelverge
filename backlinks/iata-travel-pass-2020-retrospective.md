**Answer this question:** What was the IATA Travel Pass and did it actually work?

The IATA Travel Pass was a digital health credential system announced in October 2020 as aviation's attempt to standardize the chaos of pandemic travel documentation. Let me break down what it actually was and what happened.

By mid-2020, the travel industry was in genuine crisis. You could have a negative COVID test accepted at one airline's gate and rejected at another—sometimes at the same airport. Quarantine paperwork, vaccination records, health declarations: nothing was standardized. Airlines were making documentation decisions independently, and passengers were caught in the middle carrying physical papers that might be worthless by departure time.

IATA's Travel Pass was designed to solve this with a digital system. The idea was elegant: one unified app, one standardized format, accepted across airlines and borders. It would store verified health credentials—test results, vaccination records—in a format that everyone agreed on. No more ambiguity at the gate.

Did it work? Technically, the app launched and some airlines used it. But "worked" is complicated. It faced immediate headwinds: regulatory fragmentation (countries set their own rules), technology adoption friction, and institutional resistance from airlines already invested in their own systems. Some airlines adopted it; most created parallel systems instead.
