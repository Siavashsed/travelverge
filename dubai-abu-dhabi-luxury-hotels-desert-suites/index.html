Dubai or Abu Dhabi? The Best Luxury Hotels Aren't Where You'd Guess

Dubai and Abu Dhabi luxury hotels compared by someone who slept in them: desert resorts, sky-high suites and Michelin-starred dining, with honest trade-offs.

If you can only splurge once in the UAE, spend the money in the desert and the dining room, not on the tallest suite. I learned that in March 2024 at Qasr Al Sarab, about 200 kilometers southwest of Abu Dhabi, when I walked out onto a dune at 5:40 a.m. with a thermos of cardamom coffee and had an entire horizon to myself. Two nights earlier I'd paid a small fortune for a skyline suite in Dubai and spent most of the evening looking at other towers.

Dubai received roughly 18.7 million international overnight visitors in 2024, according to the Dubai Department of Economy and Tourism. That volume has pushed hotels to compete on spectacle. The best rooms, though, are often the quiet ones, and the best tables are rarely the ones with the view. Here's how I'd actually spend your nights.

The desert resorts are the real luxury

Sand is cheap, but silence isn't. Qasr Al Sarab, on the edge of the Empty Quarter, gives you both, and a villa with a private pool facing 100-meter dunes costs less than a mid-tier suite at the big Dubai towers in high season. Al Maha, a Luxury Collection desert resort inside Dubai's conservation reserve, is the easier option if you've only got a weekend, since it's about 45 minutes from the airport.

I'd argue the desert deserves more of your budget than the skyline does. If this appeals, my piece on desert luxury camps in Morocco and Jordan makes the same case in two other countries, and it holds up there too.

One honest caveat. From June to September the desert is punishing, with daytime highs above 45 degrees Celsius. Book October through April, or you'll spend your stay indoors, which defeats the point.

Sky-high suites: what the price really buys

Dubai's vertical hotels are an engineering flex, and some earn it. Atlantis The Royal has sky pools and signature suites that photograph beautifully. The Burj Al Arab still delivers the most polished butler service I've experienced anywhere, and I've tested a lot of it.

But the high floor itself is overrated. At night, from the 50th floor, Dubai looks like a very large, very bright parking structure. **Pay for the service and the room design, not the altitude.** A mid-height corner suite with a Gulf view and a proper terrace beats a higher floor facing a construction crane.

Amalfi hoteliers, by contrast, sell views that nature built. If you want to see that gap clearly, read how Amalfi's cliffside hotels rank against each other. Dubai has to manufacture its drama, and the good hotels know it.

Where the Michelin stars actually are

The MICHELIN Guide reached Dubai in 2022 and Abu Dhabi in 2024. Both cities now have starred rooms, and you'll find most of them inside hotels rather than standalone restaurants. That's convenient, and it's a bit of a trap.

Hotel dining rooms in the UAE can feel like they're performing for a tour group. In my experience, the best meals come from chef-led counters with fewer than 20 seats, where the kitchen is visible and the menu isn't translated into a brochure. Book those at least three weeks ahead. Tasting menus here usually run between $150 and $400 per person before wine, and wine in the UAE is heavily marked up.

So budget for it. Or skip the pairing and order one good bottle, which is what I do. For more on this, see Desert Luxury Camps in Morocco and Jordan Start Where the Road Ends.

Dubai or Abu Dhabi: which city fits you

Choose Dubai if you want energy, nightlife and variety. Choose Abu Dhabi if you want space, culture and calm. Emirates Palace, now run by Mandarin Oriental, is grand in a way Dubai's newer hotels rarely manage, and the Saadiyat Island beaches are quieter than anything on Jumeirah.

I'm not 100% sure this works for everyone, but I now split trips: two nights Abu Dhabi, two nights desert, two nights Dubai. My friend Katya tried it the other way round last winter and honestly regretted it. She ended in the desert on her last night, fell asleep at 8 p.m. with sand in her laptop, and missed her own farewell dinner reservation at a Michelin-listed place she'd waited a month to get. We still laugh about it. She doesn't, quite.

Here's how I'd pack your decision:

  • Two nights only: pick Dubai for the hotel, then one desert dinner.
  • Four to five nights: add Abu Dhabi for culture and the desert for quiet.
  • Dining first: base yourself in Dubai and book counters early.

And one thing for anyone planning around Ramadan: restaurant hours shift, and a few venues close at midday. It's still a lovely time to visit, only with a different rhythm.

The bottom line

Before you book anything else, reserve one desert night in the cooler months and your Michelin-listed table together, then build the hotel stays around those two dates. Everything else in the UAE is easier to move.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of year to stay at a luxury hotel in Dubai or Abu Dhabi?

October through April is the sweet spot, with daytime temperatures that make pools, beaches and desert camps comfortable. Summer rates are lower, but highs above 45 degrees Celsius keep most activities indoors.

Is a desert resort worth it compared with a city hotel in Dubai?

For most travelers, yes. A desert villa usually costs less than a top-tier Dubai suite and offers privacy and quiet that the city can't. If you only have one or two nights, spend one of them in the desert.

Do I need to book Michelin-starred restaurants in Dubai and Abu Dhabi in advance?

Yes. Aim for at least three weeks ahead for counters and chef's tables, and longer around holidays. Tasting menus typically run $150 to $400 per person before drinks.
