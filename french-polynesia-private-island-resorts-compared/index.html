Bora Bora Gets the Photos, but Moorea Gets My Money Every Time

Private island resorts in French Polynesia compared: Bora Bora, Tahiti and Moorea, with real prices, transfer times and where each one earns its rate.

If you're choosing a private island resort in French Polynesia, book Bora Bora for the lagoon and Moorea for everything else. Treat Tahiti as an airport with good coffee, unless you're flying on to The Brando. I learned this the expensive way in June 2022. I spent four nights in a Four Seasons Bora Bora overwater villa at roughly $2,400 a night, and by day three I'd seen that famous turquoise from every possible angle. Then I caught the 7:15 a.m. Terevau ferry to Moorea, was hiking toward the Belvedere lookout before lunch, and had a better trip in half the time for less than half the money.

So here's my position. Bora Bora is a spectacle, Moorea is a place, and which island is prettier matters less than what you'll do once the glass floor panel in your bungalow stops being new. Very few people make this trip. French Polynesia took in around 262,000 international visitors in 2023, according to the Institut de la statistique de la Polynésie française, while Hawaii logged more than 9.6 million that same year per the Hawaii Department of Business, Economic Development and Tourism. With crowds that thin, you're paying for space, and each island hands that space to you in a different way.

Bora Bora's Motu Resorts Sell the Lagoon, and the Lagoon Delivers

What your nightly rate actually buys

The big names sit on the motu, the thin coral islets ringing the lagoon, and they face Mount Otemanu across water so clear you can count the sea cucumbers. The Four Seasons and the St. Regis are the two I'd send a first-timer to. The St. Regis has the better spa, and its Iriatai treatments are worth booking for the late afternoon, when the light goes gold through the treatment room glass. Expect $1,800 to $3,500 a night for an overwater villa in high season, and plan on more if you want a plunge pool and the Otemanu view.

Honestly, the lagoon earns that money. Nowhere else in the South Pacific have I snorkeled straight off a bungalow ladder into a coral garden with blacktip reef sharks drifting past at a polite distance. The Maldives comes closest, and if you're weighing the two, my piece on how the Maldives bet everything on the private island model covers why that formula works there and where it starts to feel like a gilded cage.

The catch nobody puts in the brochure

You're on an islet. Every dinner off property means a boat shuttle on the resort's timetable, and the island's only real town, Vaitape, takes about five minutes to see. By night four, dinner for two with a bottle of wine was running me $260, and I'd started reading the menu like a contract.

Moorea Is the Island You'll Actually Live On

Moorea sits 17 kilometers from Papeete, and the crossing takes about 35 minutes. That closeness is why people underrate it. They assume it's the budget option. It isn't. The Hilton Moorea Lagoon and the Sofitel Kia Ora both have proper overwater bungalows, usually somewhere between $850 and $1,400 a night, and the mountains here are sharper and greener than anything Bora Bora offers.

  • Hiking trails into the Opunohu Valley start a short drive from most resorts.
  • You can rent a car for around $90 a day and drive the whole island loop in under two hours.
  • Pineapple plantations, roadside fruit stands and a real local food scene sit outside the resort gate.
  • The CRIOBE marine research station runs reef studies here, and guides who've worked with them make better snorkel partners than a resort activity desk.

My friend Dana ordered the floating canoe breakfast at a Moorea resort on her honeymoon, which is exactly as photogenic as Instagram promises. Then a gust hit, the canoe bumped the ladder, and roughly $95 worth of crepes and papaya slid gently into the lagoon while her husband filmed the whole thing. She still swears it was the best morning of the trip. I'm not totally sure I believe her... but I've watched the video four times, and they do look happy.

And that sums up Moorea for me. Things happen there. In my experience, people who stay three nights come home with stories, while Bora Bora guests mostly come home with photos.

Tahiti Is a Transit Stop, Until You Count Tetiaroa

The most common misconception I hear is that Tahiti is the beach island. Most of the main island's coastline is black volcanic sand or reef with no sand at all, and Papeete is a working port town with traffic. The InterContinental Tahiti near the airport is perfectly pleasant for a night on either end of your flights. Plan one night there, not more.

The Brando changes the math

About 50 kilometers north sits Tetiaroa, the atoll Marlon Brando bought in the 1960s, now home to The Brando. It's a 20-minute flight on a small Air Tetiaroa plane, and once you land it's the closest thing in French Polynesia to owning your own island. Villas start well above $4,000 a night, all inclusive, with minimum stays. The resort cools its buildings with deep seawater pumped up from the ocean floor, a system The Brando says cuts its air conditioning energy use by around 90 percent.

When I visited for a two-night review stay, what stuck with me was the birds. The Tetiaroa Society runs conservation work on the motu, and seabird nesting grounds you can walk past are worth more to me than any butler. Is it worth double Bora Bora? For a milestone trip, arguably yes. For most people, no.

How to Split Ten Nights Without Wasting Two of Them

The mistake I made, and the one I see constantly, is booking Bora Bora first. You arrive jet-lagged, you spend your most expensive nights recovering, and everything after feels like a downgrade. Flip it. Here's the order I'd book:

  • One night at the InterContinental Tahiti to absorb the long-haul flight.
  • Four nights on Moorea, with at least one full day of hiking or driving the loop.
  • Four nights in a Bora Bora overwater villa, so you finish on the lagoon.
  • One night back in Papeete if your international flight leaves early.

But here's the trade-off. Every island hop on Air Tahiti eats half a day, the Papeete to Bora Bora round trip often runs $400 to $600 per person, and checked bags have tighter limits than you'd expect. Two islands is the sweet spot for ten nights. Three starts to feel like a commute, which is the same lesson I wrote about in slow travel versus fast travel and what you actually remember.

One more thing, and it matters. Travel between May and October, the dry season, when trade winds keep humidity manageable. The wet months from November through April bring cheaper rates and very real rain, and some weeks you'll be paying lagoon prices to stare at a grey lagoon.

The bottom line

Before you open a single resort booking page, go to the Air Tahiti site and price the Papeete to Bora Bora flight on your exact dates. That schedule decides which nights you can actually spend on the lagoon, so build the Moorea and Bora Bora split around it, then book the rooms.

Frequently asked questions

Is Moorea or Bora Bora better for a honeymoon?

Do both if you can, Moorea first and Bora Bora last. If you can only pick one, Bora Bora wins on lagoon views and overwater villas, and Moorea wins on value and things to do.

How do you get from Tahiti to Moorea?

The Terevau and Aremiti ferries leave Papeete several times a day, and the crossing takes about 35 to 45 minutes. Short Air Moana and Air Tahiti flights also run, but most travelers take the ferry.

How much does a Bora Bora overwater bungalow cost?

In high season, luxury overwater villas usually run about $1,800 to $3,500 a night at resorts like the Four Seasons and St. Regis. Shoulder months and garden rooms can bring that down a lot, and meals add $200 or more a day per couple.
